## What "free" actually means in this category

Almost every inventory app calls itself free. The differences are in what's free:

- **Free forever, capped by scale** — free until you hit a limit (products, transactions, or team members), then you pay. This is the most common model.
- **Free forever, capped by feature** — unlimited products and sales, but reporting, exports, or multi-device sync sit behind a paywall.
- **Genuinely free, ad- or upsell-supported** — no caps, monetized some other way (or not really monetized, run as a side project or loss leader for a bigger product).

None of these is a scam — they're just different bets on what you'll eventually pay for. The mistake is picking based on the word "free" in the App Store listing instead of reading which model you're actually getting into.

## The four things that actually differentiate these apps

**1. Does it work without a connection?**
A lot of "small business" inventory apps are web apps wrapped in a native shell, which means every screen — adding stock, ringing up a sale — needs a live connection to their server. If your shop has patchy signal (a basement stockroom, a market stall, a power cut that takes the router with it), this isn't a minor inconvenience, it's the app being unusable exactly when you need it. We wrote a full breakdown of this in [how to track stock without internet](/blogs/offline-inventory-management-no-internet) — it's the single biggest practical differentiator in this category, and most comparison articles don't even mention it because most apps fail the test.

**2. Does it show you profit, or just sales?**
Sales tracking is the easy 80%. Profit tracking means the app knows your cost price per item, not just your sell price, and does the subtraction for you on every transaction. A lot of "inventory apps" are really just sales-logging apps with a stock count bolted on — useful, but they'll tell you "you sold ₹40,000 today" without ever answering "how much of that was actually yours." See our [profit margin guide](/blogs/how-to-calculate-profit-margin-small-shop) if you want to check by hand whether an app's numbers add up.

**3. Can you get your data out?**
Export to CSV/Excel isn't a nice-to-have — it's what lets your accountant do their job, what lets you switch apps later without losing years of history, and what lets you cross-check the app's math against your own. If an app doesn't have a visible, one-tap export, that's a real limitation, not a rounding error.

**4. What happens to setup time?**
The honest answer for most of these apps is: you're typing in your entire stock list by hand, once, no matter which one you pick. The apps that make this bearable have fast add-item flows (photo + name + price + quantity, nothing else required up front) rather than a 12-field form before you can save a single item.

## How to actually decide

Install two or three candidates, and before you touch a single setting, do this: add five real products with your real cost and sell prices, record three real transactions, then try to export the result. That fifteen-minute test tells you more than any comparison article, including this one — you'll immediately feel whether the app is fighting you or getting out of your way.
